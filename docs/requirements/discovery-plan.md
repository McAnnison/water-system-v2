# Requirements Discovery Plan

## Goal
Understand current operations, identify control gaps, and convert validated needs into testable requirements.

## Methods
- Interviews with process owners and staff.
- Observation or walkthroughs of production, stock, dispatch, and reconciliation.
- Review existing forms, spreadsheets, reports, and approval records.
- Map normal workflows, exceptions, corrections, and end-of-day close.
- Playback findings with stakeholders for validation.

## Questions to answer
### Business context
What products and package sizes exist? Which locations and teams participate? Which records are authoritative?

### Production
Who plans and records production? Which inputs and outputs are measured, in what units? How are wastage, rejected products, rework, and corrections handled? Who verifies records?

### Inventory
Where is stock held? How are receipts, transfers, adjustments, damage, returns, and counts recorded? How are discrepancies investigated and approved?

### Dispatch and field operations
Who requests, prepares, authorizes, and receives dispatches? What confirms delivery or return? How are shortages, partial deliveries, damaged goods, and cash collections handled?

### Finance
Which financial events are in scope? Who records, reviews, and approves them? What reconciliation evidence is required?

### People and access
What job functions exist? Which actions and records does each need? Who can approve, reverse, lock, or correct records? How are joiners, leavers, and compromised accounts handled?

### Technology and operations
Which devices and browsers are used? Where is connectivity unreliable? What data must migrate? What are availability, backup, recovery, and support expectations?

## Outputs
Stakeholder register; current/future-state workflows; glossary and business rules; exception catalogue; role-permission matrix; prioritized functional and non-functional requirements; acceptance criteria; traceability matrix; approved SRS.

## Requirement quality
Each requirement must be uniquely identified, unambiguous, necessary, feasible, verifiable, and traceable. Record unknowns explicitly rather than treating assumptions as facts.
